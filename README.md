**Emberkeeper** is an original arena-survival game: you are the keeper of the
last lantern in a dark ember-garden. Gloom-moths drift in from the dark; burst
them with embers, gather their light, and light all seven lanterns before dawn.

## How to play

- **WASD / arrows** — move the keeper
- **Mouse** — aim · **Click / Space** — shoot embers
- **E** — Lantern Flare: spend 5 shards from the lantern meter to burst every
  moth and gloom orb nearby (8s cooldown) — safety bought with progress
- **1 / 2 / 3 or click a card** — pick a difficulty on the title screen, or
  pick an upgrade between nights
- **4 / 5 / 6 or click a road** — pick a road to the next night: every road
  has its toll and its gift (see below)
- **P / Esc** — pause · **M** — mute · **R** — restart

Each night has a shard quota: fill the lantern meter and the next lantern
lights — and every night has its own name and character. Night 1 is the First
Dark; night 2 the Wisp Tide, when nearly a third of the garden is wisps, and
golden lantern-moths start appearing — they flee from you, so chase them down
for their 5 shards; night 3 the Matriarch Wakes; night 4 is Spitter's Watch,
when spitters double; night 5 the Swarm Night, the garden spawning faster with
more moths at once; night 6 the Matriarch Returns, calling darters instead of
moths; night 7 the Dawn Rush. Between nights, choose 1 of 3 blessings (faster
embers, tri-flame, piercing embers, swift boots, wide net, heavy embers, warm
heart, Ember Nova, Ember Echo, Lantern Ward, Ember Orbit, Ember Chain). From
night 2, quick darting Gloom Wisps weave in among the moths and darters; from night 3, bloated
Gloom Splitters burst into two fast motes when killed — shoot them at range or
deal with both — and dark-violet Gloom Spitters hold at range and spit slow
gloom orbs at you, telegraphing each shot with a flare: step aside, or shoot
the orb down; from night 5, slow old Elders drift in, tough at 3 hp but heavy
with 4 shards. Every 3rd night a Gloom Matriarch boss guards the lantern — and
below half health she enrages, moving faster and calling moths sooner; below a
quarter she bursts in fury, firing one ring of gloom orbs in every direction —
dodge it or shoot the orbs down. Keeper carries three hearts; a touch costs one, unless a
Lantern Ward eats the hit (Rookie carries four). Light all 7
lanterns and dawn breaks — you win. Lose all hearts and the light goes out.
After a win, Space keeps playing endless nights: the modifiers keep
rotating (Swarm, Wisp Tide, Spitter's Watch), a boss guards every 3rd night,
and the garden never wins for you again — it only gets harder.

Score comes from bursts (with a combo multiplier for quick successive kills),
shards, and night bonuses; clearing a night without losing a heart pays a
Flawless +250. The victory and game-over screens show your ember accuracy —
hits over embers fired. The best score is kept in the browser, along with the
last three runs. Hold the
combo at its max and the lantern surges — embers hit +1 harder for a few
seconds. The last night is the Dawn Rush: the garden spawns faster and the
moth cap rises, so hold the line until dawn.

## The road to the next night

After every cleared night you pick two things: one blessing (above) and one
of three roads to the next night — each with its toll written out and its gift
written out, and each really changing the night ahead:

- **Ember Shrine** — risk: the next night spawns 15% faster; gift: mend 1
  heart now and +150 score.
- **Quiet Path** — no toll, no gift; the garden rests.
- **Moth Market** — risk: the next lantern needs +6 shards; gift: +5 shards
  pre-lit on it and +250 score.

The seven nights cross four realms — Hedge Row (nights 1–2), Old Pond (3–4),
Ember Orchard (5–6), Dawn Gate (night 7) — and the road screen shows where
you are on the map. Every cleared night earns a grade from the hits you took
(S for untouched, A for one hit, B for two, C for three or more), and the dawn
screen lists all seven grades, the run counted ten ways (score, moths burst,
accuracy, embers fired, flawless nights, Nova fires, chain hits, Surge
triggers, orbs shot down, time), and the ten feats: First Light, Flawless Keeper, Nova
Storm, Chain Master, Warden, Mote Hunter, Orb Breaker, Sharpshooter,
Dawnbringer and Full Hearts — a feat you have not earned yet shows how
close you came (42/50 motes, 29%/40% accuracy), so the dawn screen reads
as a list of near-misses to chase, not dead slots. The play HUD shows the stakes (WIN light 7
lanterns · LOSE all 3 hearts), what is alive in the garden right now by kind
(the census wraps to a second line on a busy night instead of spilling
past its panel), and what the next night will be.

The title screen plays the game live while you read: a framed LIVE preview
shows the keeper fighting alone under the autopilot (nearest moth, kite,
gather shards) with a live tally — moths burst, score, shards gathered —
and the manual beside it, so the first screen is a game being played, not a
poster. The victory and game-over screens name the next goal — Inferno for
a Keeper win, the first unearned feat for an Inferno win, the win itself
for a run that ended early — so every ending points at the next run.

## Difficulties

Pick your keeping on the title screen (press 1 / 2 / 3 or click a card); the
choice is remembered between visits. Only three numbers change — the garden,
the nights, the boss and the upgrades are the same game on every tier:

- **Rookie** — the garden spawns at 0.8× the rate, the keeper carries 4
  hearts, score pays the base amount. For learning the garden.
- **Keeper** — the intended game: full spawn rate, 3 hearts, base score.
- **Inferno** — the garden spawns at 1.25× and every point pays 1.5× score,
  still 3 hearts. For keepers who want the garden at its worst and the score
  to prove it.

The HUD names the tier and its score rate, and the victory and game-over
screens record which tier the run was played on. Local autopilot measurement
(15 seeds, first-pick upgrades): Keeper 15/15 wins, Rookie 15/15, Inferno
14/15 — hard, not impossible.

## The game

- A complete loop: title → 7 named nights with escalating moths, darters,
  wisps, splitters, motes, elders, spitters and fleeing lantern-moths →
  an enraging boss who changes what she summons and bursts in fury at low
  health → 12 upgrade choices + 3 roads between nights, each night graded
  S/A/B/C → victory or game-over screen with 10 feats and the next goal →
  restart / endless. Three difficulties (Rookie / Keeper / Inferno) scale only the
  spawn rate, the hearts and the score rate. Each night has its own name and twist: First Dark, Wisp Tide, the
  Matriarch Wakes, Spitter's Watch, Swarm Night, the Matriarch Returns, and
  the Dawn Rush finale.
- Original art drawn in code (no images) and original sound synthesised with
  WebAudio (no audio files): shoot, burst, pickup, hurt, lantern, upgrade,
  boss, nova, ward, spit, flare, fury, victory and defeat cues over a soft ambient pad.
- No libraries, no network, no build step: plain static files under `game/`,
  playable offline in a desktop browser from `game/index.html`.

## Run it

Node 22 or later, standard library only:

```sh
node serve.ts        # serves game/ on http://localhost:8080
```

Or open `game/index.html` directly in a browser.

## Files

- `main.ts` — hands the entry in: copies `game/` + `screenshots/` to
  `/output` and writes `/output/dossier.json` via `vendor/makefun-game.ts`.
- `game/index.html` — the page; `game/src/` — one job per file:
  `config.js` (tuning + palette + upgrades + roads + feats + realms), `random.js` (seeded RNG),
  `audio.js` (synth SFX + pad), `input.js` (keyboard + mouse),
  `world.js` (entities, nights, boss, upgrades, roads, grades, feats, collisions, scoring),
  `render.js` (garden, keeper, moths, HUD, screens), `shots.js` (staged
  screenshot scenes, played by an autopilot on the real rules),
  `main.js` (boot + fixed-step loop + screens).
- `vendor/makefun-game.ts` — the kit's dossier library, unchanged.
- `screenshots/` — 1-start.jpg (title), 2-play.jpg (night play + HUD),
  3-upgrade.jpg (blessing + road choice), 4-dawn.jpg (victory: grades, stats, feats).
- `LICENSE` — MIT.

## Notes

- `?seed=N` fixes the garden's pattern; `?shot=start|play|upgrade|win`
  stages a scene for a screenshot (the same seed always gives the same frame).
- The program is deterministic: two runs write identical bytes.
